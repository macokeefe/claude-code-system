TUUCIMeritage 3-Seater · Bottlenecks

Where the time concentrates

Three assemblies dominate — and each is bigger than takt

Step timeOver taktCumulative %– – takt 42 min
  • Step 4 — Arms Assembly: 64 min. A quarter of the whole build, and 1.5× the takt by itself.
  • Step 5 — Seat Frame Assembly: 47 min and Step 6 — Back Frame Assembly: 43.5 min.
  • These three steps = 61% of the labor. Fix these and you move the line.
  • All three exceed the 42-min takt, so as a single-person task they cap output no matter how you staff the rest.

Takt = available time ÷ demand = 420 min (7 hr) ÷ 10 units = 42 min per unit, the pace we must match.

TUUCIMeritage 3-Seater · The Opportunity

Where we can help

Put hands where the hours are

The three big assemblies are buildable in parallel — more than one person can work them at once. Splitting them is what brings every station under takt.

#4 Arms Assembly6432
#5 Seat Frame Assembly4723.5

Minutes per unit. Splitting a task only helps where the work is genuinely separable — these assemblies are.

  • The principle: a step's labor doesn't disappear when you split it — but the time on the clock drops, so it stops gating the line.
  • Arms (64 → 32) alone takes the worst bottleneck below takt.
  • The smaller steps (legs, attach, finishing, plate) are grouped to fill the remaining operators evenly.
  • Net effect on the next slide: a level line that hits 10/day.

TUUCIMeritage 3-Seater · The Balance

The line balance

From lumpy to level

Today — work as it sits

One operator per step: Arms towers at 64 min. The line can only release a unit every 64 min → ≈6.6 units/day, and the tiny steps sit idle.

Proposed — balanced to takt

7 operators across 6 stations (Arms built 2-up; Seat & Back Frame split). Every station under the 42-min takt → ≈10.1 units/day, 88% utilized.

TUUCIMeritage 3-Seater · Summary

What this means

Same product, leveled to demand

  • 254 min of labor, with 61% in three assemblies — Arms (#4), Seat Frame (#5), Back Frame (#6).
  • Those three each run longer than takt, so they’re the constraint.
  • Build Arms 2-up, split Seat & Back Frame, group the rest → 7 operators across 6 stations, every station ≤ 41.5 min.
  • Result: ~10.1 units/day at 88% efficiency, meeting the 10/day demand.
Assumptions & caveats. Demand 10/day over a 7-hour day → 42-min takt. Times are measured from the standard work instruction. Splitting helps only where a task is genuinely separable; sequence-locked work won’t overlap — to be confirmed on the floor.
Next step. Validate the three split assemblies on the floor, then trial the 7-station layout. The analyzer lets us re-balance live as real times firm up.
