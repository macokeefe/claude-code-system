About WaterAware

Air quality has IQAir; drinking water has… buried PDF reports and agency databases. WaterAware is a prototype that turns official water-quality records into one map with one simple score, so anyone can see at a glance how the water where they live is doing.

🚰 Tap water — where the data comes from

Every US public water system must report violations of the Safe Drinking Water Act to the EPA. This view queries the EPA ECHO Safe Drinking Water services directly from your browser and shows each community water system, sized by the population it serves. EPA refreshes this data quarterly — it is a compliance record, not a live sensor.

🚰 How the tap-water score works

Every system starts at 100 and loses points for problems in EPA's record (the last 3–5 years, per field):

· Serious violator designation (unresolved serious, multiple, or continuing violations): −45
· Health-based violations (contaminant above legal limit, or treatment failure): −20
· Quarters in violation over the past 3 years: −2 each (up to −24)
· Quarters as serious violator: −2 each (up to −12)
· Lead / copper action-level exceedances (5 yr): −8 each (up to −16)
· Current unresolved violation: −8
· Monitoring / reporting failures: −5  ·  Public-notice failures: −5

The result is clamped to 0–100. A 100 means EPA's record shows no recent violations — it does not certify the water is perfect; monitoring gaps and unregulated contaminants (like many PFAS) don't show up here.

🌊 Rivers & streams — live source water

The second tab shows live sensor readings from USGS real-time stations on rivers, lakes and streams — the source much of your tap water starts as. Its 0–100 score blends dissolved oxygen, turbidity, pH, specific conductance and temperature (NSF-inspired weights), computed in your browser from the USGS Instantaneous Values API.

Awareness tool, not a lab report. Tap-water scores reflect regulatory compliance history, not a chemical analysis of what's in your glass today — a compliant system can still have aging pipes in your building, and violations can take a quarter to appear. For your system's measured contaminant levels, read its annual Consumer Confidence Report or the EPA report linked in each popup.

Why US-only (for now)

The US is unusual in publishing per-system compliance data through a public API. A global version would add the UN's GEMStat archive, WHO/UNICEF JMP access data, and satellite-derived source-water indicators.
