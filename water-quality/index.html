About WaterAware

Air quality has IQAir; water quality has… scattered agency portals. WaterAware is a prototype that pulls live sensor readings from thousands of USGS real-time monitoring stations on US rivers, lakes and streams, and turns them into one simple 0–100 score so anyone can see at a glance how the water near them is doing.

Where the data comes from

Every reading comes straight from the USGS Instantaneous Values API in your browser — no server, no accounts, updated by USGS as often as every 15 minutes. Stations are loaded for whatever area you pan or zoom to.

How the score works

The score is a weighted blend of up to five live parameters, inspired by the NSF Water Quality Index. Each parameter is converted to a 0–100 subscore, then combined (weights renormalized to whichever parameters a station actually reports):

· Dissolved oxygen 00300 — weight 30%. Low oxygen suffocates aquatic life.
· Turbidity 63680 — weight 25%. Cloudy water carries sediment, runoff and pathogens.
· pH 00400 — weight 20%. Healthy rivers sit near 7–8.
· Specific conductance 00095 — weight 15%. A proxy for dissolved salts and pollution.
· Water temperature 00010 — weight 10%. Hot water holds less oxygen and stresses ecosystems.

A station needs at least two of these parameters reporting to receive a score; otherwise it appears as a small grey dot you can still click to see its raw readings.

This is an awareness tool, not a safety rating. These are ambient surface-water sensors — they say nothing about your tap water, and they don't measure bacteria, lead, PFAS or nitrates, which require lab testing. Never use this map to judge whether water is safe to drink or swim in.

Why US-only (for now)

The US is the only country with a free, dense, real-time public water-sensor network. A global version would layer in the UN's GEMStat archive, satellite-derived turbidity/algae from programs like GEO AquaWatch, and eventually crowdsourced test strips — the same path air quality took from government monitors to PurpleAir.
